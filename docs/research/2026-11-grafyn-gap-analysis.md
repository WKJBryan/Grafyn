# Grafyn Gap Analysis — What the Research Literature Says

**Date:** November 2026 · **Status:** research report, no code changes implied by this document alone

**Scope.** This report maps published evidence onto Grafyn's four core mechanisms: (1) the digital twin (context-constructed individual decision prediction), (2) branch-dream conversation compaction, (3) graph-augmented temporal retrieval, and (4) sealed-prediction capture. It respects two standing owner decisions: **in-app accuracy scoring stays out** (capture + export only; scoring lives in the external harness), and the **2026-09-05 evidence-ingestion redesign is proposed but unbuilt** — implications below that overlap it are marked. Claims were gathered via literature search in November 2026; numbers sourced from secondary summaries rather than paper tables are flagged in [Caveats](#caveats).

## 1. Predicting an individual's decisions

The headline result validating Grafyn's whole architecture: **"Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (Park et al., arXiv 2411.10109, 2024)**. Agents built from a ~2-hour open-ended interview predicted each person's General Social Survey answers at **85% normalized accuracy** — normalized against the fact that people only matched their *own* answers 81% of the time two weeks later. Demographic agents scored 0.71 and machine-written persona descriptions 0.70. Implication: the user's own verbatim open-ended narrative (decision rationales, interview-like material) is the highest-value capture, and raw accuracy is meaningless without a self-consistency denominator — which is exactly what implication #4 (`repeat_of`) makes measurable from exports.

**Twin-2K-500 (arXiv 2505.17479, 2025)** — the best LLM twins conditioned on full prior survey answers hit ~71.7% on held-out questions (vs 59% random, 82% human test-retest), and **fine-tuning on 500 examples did worse (69.6%) than in-context persona text**. This is direct evidence that Grafyn's frozen-LLM + context-construction design is the *stronger* choice at personal data scale, not a compromise. The follow-up **mega-study on the same panel (19 pre-registered sub-studies)** found twins systematically *less variable* than their humans — regression-to-the-stereotype — so capturing disagreement signals (corrections, regret, rejected options) matters as much as modal choices.

**SynthesizeMe! (ACL 2025)** and **PersonaJudge (SIGDIAL 2026)** both show in-context demonstrations of a *specific* person's actual judgments carry individual signal that persona text misses — and SynthesizeMe's best method derives the persona *from* the demonstrations. This confirms the roadmap's center-of-gravity bet (case library + contrastive pairs from existing ranking/feedback events) and the Constitution's receipts rule (rules co-presented with verbatim excerpts, in `build_twin_context_prompt()` in `commands/canvas/context.rs`).

**CoMPosT (EMNLP 2023)** measured caricature in persona simulation: models exaggerate the most salient identity traits. For Grafyn: Simulation mode will drift toward a caricature of the Constitution's loudest rules unless concrete, situation-specific episodes dilute the persona label — one more reason cases outrank abstractions in the prompt order. Persona-drift work (**arXiv 2402.10962, 2024**) shows prompted personas decay within ~8 dialogue rounds; Grafyn's per-hop re-injection of Twin Identity + Constitution is the right countermeasure and should be locked in by a test.

**"LLMs Do Not Simulate Human Psychology" (arXiv 2508.06950, 2025)** and **Lin's "Six Fallacies" (AMPPS 2025)** both conclude simulations must be re-validated per application against the real human. Grafyn's sealed-prediction + outcome capture is therefore not optional polish — it's the only accuracy claim the literature permits. Finally, **hypothetical-bias meta-analyses (List & Gallet 2001; Murphy et al. 2005)** quantify that stated preferences overstate real ones (median ratio 1.35–3×): decision episodes with actual choices and outcomes are categorically stronger evidence than "what would you prefer" answers, which supports the Phase 2 / ingestion-spec evidence-class weighting and the `observed_action` vs `hypothetical_response` case-kind split.

**Free text alone is modest signal:** an LLM given a user's social-media posts picked their actual preference in only 63% of binary trials (ACM 2025). The vault justifies vault-as-context but cannot alone reach interview-level accuracy — structured decision episodes must carry the rest.

## 2. Branch-dream compaction

Grafyn's pattern — summary document + last-2-verbatim-turns, compact on demand at ~32k — matches the main-context design of **MemGPT (arXiv 2310.08560, 2023)** and is directionally validated by **"Beyond Goldfish Memory" (Xu, Szlam & Weston, ACL 2022)**, where summary-conditioned models beat full-raw-history models for multi-session dialogue. But two gaps are well-documented:

- **No recall path.** MemGPT's dramatic deep-memory-retrieval gain (~32% → ~92.5%, secondary figure) comes from the half Grafyn lacks: paging evicted verbatim turns back in. Grafyn runs the *unevaluated* half of MemGPT (summary + recency buffer alone has, as far as found, never been published as a standalone eval — see §5). Keeping compacted turns searchable (even just on disk) is the biggest evidence-backed structural gap; a session-trace search already partially exists in `services/twin/traces.rs` and could be the substrate.
- **Summary staleness / lost corrections.** The recursive-summary dialogue paper (arXiv 2308.15022) names the failure mode exactly: outdated information persisting in summary memory with no revision path. **Mem0 (arXiv 2504.19413, 2025)** — the field's current production pattern — gets +26% relative accuracy on LoCoMo from structured fact extraction with explicit ADD/UPDATE/DELETE semantics instead of free-text summaries. Grafyn's dream is prose: "actually, I'm vegetarian" survives only until the next compile. Implication #5: a small standing-facts/corrections list inside `CanvasWorkingMemory` with update-on-conflict.

**The compounding path is real in Grafyn's code.** `working_memory.rs` recompiles incrementally from the *previous dream + turns since last compile* (`turns_after_compiled_from`, test `incremental_compile_prompt_contains_previous_goal_and_later_turns_only`). **"LLM as a Broken Telephone" (ACL 2025)** shows factual fidelity degrades monotonically across iterative re-generation, and **Wu et al. (arXiv 2109.10862, 2021)** located recursive summarization as the locus of upward error propagation. The design choice is defensible (cost), but it should carry a decay regression test: plant N facts, compact twice, probe recall (#6).

**Dialogue is the worst summarization genre.** TofuEval (NAACL 2024) and ACL 2024 work found >30% of LLM dialogue summaries contain inconsistencies, with entity/number errors a named category — and Grafyn compiles with a deliberately cheap model. A post-compile check that entities/numbers in the dream appear in source turns (#7) targets the best-documented corruption mode cheaply.

**What to test, not assume:** "Lost in the Middle" (Liu et al., TACL 2024) means sub-threshold full-transcript sends are not automatically safe — mid-transcript facts are the likeliest misses — and Chroma's Context Rot report (2025) shows degradation accelerates with semantically similar distractors, which is precisely what multi-model tiles produce (many near-duplicate answers). **LongMemEval (ICLR 2025)** provides the failure-mode checklist for any external eval of this path: temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention are the hard categories, and the dream currently has no timestamps, no superseded-fact marking, and no abstention basis. The Generative Agents ablations (UIST 2023) also showed a periodic reflection pass adds measurable quality — Grafyn has retrieval and a summary but no reflection step; a cheap "what did the user decide / correct / rule out" field appended at compile time is the evidence-aligned version and dovetails with #5.

## 3. Graph-augmented and temporal retrieval

What the evidence supports in Grafyn's pipeline: **BM25 as the base** (BEIR, NeurIPS 2021: dense retrievers often underperform BM25 zero-shot out-of-domain — a personal vault is maximally out-of-domain), **LLM query rewriting** (Ma et al., EMNLP 2023; HyDE, ACL 2023 — rewriting mainly rescues vocabulary mismatch, which is BM25's exact weakness), and **graph expansion as a recall device** (citation-expansion literature: neighbor expansion raises recall, costs precision, and the standard remedy is expansion-then-rerank).

What's missing, in evidence order:

1. **Reranking** (#9): monoT5-class cross-encoders lift TREC-DL nDCG@10 from ~0.49 to ~0.71–0.75 — the largest single-stage gain in the survey, and it slots after the existing top-K with no index changes. Without a rerank/quality gate after N-hop expansion, expansion is as likely to dilute context as enrich it.
2. **Contextual chunk prefixes** (#10): Anthropic's 2024 contextual-retrieval numbers (top-20 failure rate 5.7% → 2.9% with contextual BM25 alone) are achievable mostly *without* an LLM — prepend title/section-path to each TextTiling chunk at index time.
3. **Hybrid BM25+dense via RRF** (#11) when `SimilarityProvider` lands (roadmap Phase 3): typical double-digit-percent lift over BM25 alone, but one 2025 study shows fusion can hurt when one retriever is already strong — measure on vault data, don't assume.
4. **PPR over the existing wikilink graph** (#13): HippoRAG (NeurIPS 2024) validated Personalized PageRank seeded from query-matched nodes — up to +20 recall points on multi-hop QA, *comparable to baselines on single-hop*. Grafyn gets the graph for free (user-authored), so the HippoRAG mechanism without the KG-construction cost is plausible — but gate it by query type; every controlled RAG-vs-GraphRAG comparison (arXiv 2502.11371; GraphRAG-Bench 2025) shows graph methods tie or lose on single-fact queries while adding latency.

**Temporal:** Zep (arXiv 2501.13956, 2025) showed bitemporal fact validity gives its gains specifically on temporal-reasoning (+17pts) and multi-session questions — supporting the roadmap's Phase 4 (wire `valid_from`/`valid_until`) rather than recency boosts. Time-aware ranking literature supports recency only as a *bounded, blended* prior; pure/heavy date reordering hurts relevance.

**Chunking:** "Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?" (arXiv 2410.13070, 2024) found fixed-size chunking consistently beat semantic/breakpoint chunking on realistic data (Vectara-authored caveat). Don't invest further in TextTiling sophistication; contextual prefixes are the better spend.

## 4. Sealed-prediction capture and calibration

The roadmap's sealing design is directionally right; the literature sharpens the *schema*:

- **Proper scoring rules** (Brier 1950; Gneiting & Raftery, JASA 2007): top-1 agreement is an improper score — it can't separate calibration from resolution and rewards hedging. Without the full per-option probability vector logged at seal time, the export is *permanently lossy*: Brier/log scores and calibration curves can never be computed retroactively (#1).
- **Confidence elicitation** (Tian et al., EMNLP 2023; Xiong et al., ICLR 2024): verbalized confidence beats token probabilities for RLHF models but clusters overconfidently at 80–100% in multiples of 5; sampling consistency (vote share over k samples) is the better signal, hybrid is best. Schema: log the exact elicitation prompt + model + temperature; reserve `n_samples`/per-option vote-count fields so consistency confidence can be added without a schema break. Never display raw verbalized confidence as "the twin is 90% sure."
- **Outcome switching is the dominant real-world leakage mode** (COMPare, Trials 2019: ~31% of registered trials changed primary outcomes post-registration). Freeze question + options at seal; hash the sealed payload into the export so the harness can detect drift; a post-seal edit voids the episode (#2).
- **Forecasting-tournament logging** (Mellers et al., Psych Science 2014; Metaculus question standards): timestamped probabilities, resolution criteria fixed ex ante, re-seals as new rows never edits; flag episodes resolved as "none of the listed options" as unresolvable rather than scoring them.
- **Influence/leakage** (Logg et al., OBHDP 2019 — people adjust *more* toward algorithmic advice; Fogliato et al., FAccT 2022 — AI-first ordering anchors experts): the planned `initial_leaning`-before-render capture is directly supported; add `twin_answer_visible_before_choice` + answer render timestamp so the harness can condition on visibility (#3).
- **Small-N statistics** (Brown, Cai & DasGupta, Statistical Science 2001; Simmons et al., Psych Science 2011): Wilson/Jeffreys intervals only; at n=50 with 80% observed agreement the 95% CI still contains 70% — distinguishing 80% from 70% needs ~100–150 resolved episodes; and since the owner will watch accuracy accumulate, the app should show counts + interval, never a running "significant?" verdict (optional-stopping inflation). This matches the roadmap's Statistical Honesty section with citations attached.
- **The ceiling** (DCE test-retest studies, ~57–82% self-consistency depending on horizon; Park et al.'s 81% two-week figure): without disguised repeat items (`repeat_of`, #4), "the twin is 75% accurate" is uninterpretable.

## 5. What the evidence does NOT support

- **Relation-type edge weights as retrieval multipliers** (`supports: 1.5x`, etc., in `RelationWeights`): no study found measures gains from semantic edge-type weighting; GraphRAG/HippoRAG treat edges uniformly. Keep the typed graph for UX and explanations; treat the multipliers as needing in-house A/B or flattening to 1.0.
- **Hub-connectivity boosts** (`hub_boost_weight`): mixed-to-negative evidence — indegree lowered MAP in one study; Richardson et al. (WWW 2006) found little evidence PageRank-style static link scores help ranking. Weakest-evidenced stage in the pipeline; demote behind an ablation.
- **Always-on multi-hop expansion**: every controlled comparison shows graph methods tie/lose on single-fact queries. Gate by query type.
- **LLM-extracted knowledge-graph construction** for a personal vault: validated only on ~1M-token corpora for global sensemaking at high cost; the user-authored wikilink graph is exempt (free), but building an *additional* extracted KG is not evidenced.
- **Trait/personality questionnaires as prompts**: predicted traits retrieve irrelevant preferences and can reduce accuracy; richer trait text adds ~nothing over demographics. Grafyn correctly has no Big-Five intake — don't add one. (The roadmap already ranks its trait-vector item #9 "least promising" — the literature agrees and goes further.)
- **Machine-written persona summaries as primary representation**: 0.70 vs 0.85 for interview-grounded agents in Park et al. Summaries derived from the user's own data may be acceptable *compression* (two 2026 abstracts report structured profiles matching transcripts), but a summary *in place of* raw material loses the signal — this is the literature's version of the roadmap's receipts rule.
- **Fine-tuning at personal data scale**: Twin-2K's 500-example fine-tune underperformed in-context conditioning.
- **Trusting raw verbalized confidence**: systematically inflated and quantized; log it, recalibrate externally, never surface it uncalibrated.
- **Heavy recency reordering** and **Ebbinghaus-style forgetting decay** (MemoryBank's curve was never ablated): recency is a bounded tiebreaker, not a ranking driver — consistent with the roadmap demoting recency decay.
- **"Summary + last-2-turns" as a proven pattern**: ubiquitous in practice, never published as a standalone eval; Grafyn runs the unevaluated half of MemGPT. The `k=2` verbatim-turn constant is folklore — worth an internal sweep.
- **"Passes as you" claims**: Turing-test results use generic personas; no published study tests whether people who know a specific person can distinguish their twin. No such claim should appear in product copy.

## Caveats

Findings were gathered via web search (November 2026). Paper identities, venues, and qualitative conclusions were verified by search; several **headline numbers came from secondary summaries, not paper tables**, and should be re-verified before quoting publicly: MemGPT's 32%→92.5% DMR split, Park et al.'s raw 69%/81% figures, LoCoMo human-gap percentages, HippoRAG 2 / Zep per-category scores, HyDE tables, Elastic's +18% RRF and Anthropic's −67% (vendor self-reports), DCE retest percentages, and COMPare's ~31% outcome-switching rate. The ~100–150-episode power figure is standard binomial math, not a citation. Two venue attributions (PersonaJudge/SIGDIAL 2026, the Twin-2K mega-study) rest on single search results.
